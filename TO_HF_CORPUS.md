## 0. Guiding principle

To our knowledge the **first public timsTOF DDA-PASEF PSM resource** of
this kind (claim to be backed by a documented PRIDE/Zenodo/HF search in the
dataset card, or softened — §11). Intended as a **reference corpus** many
models can train from — not one team's opinionated training split. The
rule that follows:

> **Include broadly, filter minimally at build, label richly, document the
> defaults.** Every hard filter is information irreversibly destroyed;
> every exposed score is a filter the user can apply themselves.

So the corpus ships at a single minimal floor and carries every confidence/
quality signal as a column. Users wanting a stricter set filter *up*; users
wanting to recompute FDR or study borderline IDs are served by a later
decoy companion (§3.2). **We never ship a number a user could not have
recomputed from what we shipped** — and we never claim a statistical
guarantee the build doesn't actually establish (§3.2).

What makes it novel (vs MassIVE-KB / ProteomeTools / PROSPECT):
1. dual-engine (Sage + FragPipe) PSM-level **agreement labels**,
2. per-precursor **RT and IM distribution widths** (FWHM, σ, skew, R²) —
   not just apex point estimates,
3. per-precursor **per-PASEF-event collision energies** (raw volts),
4. (Tier 2, deferred) raw MS1 marginal traces + raw 4D point cloud,
5. dual-engine **matched-fragment tables** with cross-engine agreement.

## 3. Filtering & certainty policy (the core of this plan)

Answers explicitly: *which peptides/ions are kept, on what statistical
grounds, and what is labeled vs gated.*

### 3.1 The two-level model: gate minimally, label maximally

- **Gated at build** (rows that don't exist): only the hard floor below.
- **Labeled** (kept, with a column to filter on): all confidence scores,
  quality metrics, engine-agreement, width-fit reliability — §3.7.

### 3.2 PSM-level floor (per-engine q ≤ 0.01) — and what it does/doesn't mean

A row enters Tier 1 iff, in **at least one** engine, the identification is
**rank-1**, **target**, and at **that engine's reported q ≤ 0.01**.

> ⚠️ **This is a union of two engine-specific 1%-FDR sets, NOT a
> corpus-level 1%-FDR guarantee.** Each engine controls FDR within its own
> search (own decoys, scoring, calibration); their union has no single
> nominal FDR. The card states the floor as *"accepted by ≥1 engine at that
> engine's reported q ≤ 0.01"* — never "the corpus is 1% FDR." A
> calibrated union/consensus FDR (shared decoys) is a possible v0.2 add-on,
> not claimed now.

Then **expose** (never re-gate at build): `sage_qvalue`, `sage_pep`,
`sage_hyperscore`, `fragpipe_qvalue`, `fragpipe_pep`,
`fragpipe_probability`, `fragpipe_hyperscore`, plus the per-engine status
columns (§3.4) and `n_engines`.

> **What users can and can't do with the exposed scores.** Filtering to
> `q ≤ 0.001` **applies a stricter reported-q threshold** under each
> engine's original calibration — it is *not* a recomputation of FDR, and
> post-hoc combinations (agreement ∧ PTM ∧ charge ∧ quality filters) do
> **not** retain a nominal FDR. The card says "apply stricter reported-q
> thresholds," not "tighten FDR freely." Genuine FDR recomputation needs
> the decoy companion (§10).

**Verify (build-time assertion):** every exported q-value is **PSM-level**,
not peptide- or protein-level (Sage and FragPipe both surface PSM q — confirm
the canonical-parquet column is the PSM one).

**Engine-specific PEP:** ship `sage_pep` and `fragpipe_pep` separately,
present only when that engine identified the PSM (else **null** = "this
engine did not identify this precursor," which is itself information — not
zero, not imputed). ⚠️ The two PEPs are **complementary but correlated**:
both score the *same* spectrum and may share FASTA/feature inputs. Document
that they **must not be multiplied or averaged into a consensus
probability** — treat them as two correlated opinions, not independent
evidence.

**Decoys:** v0.1 is targets-only (keeps the headline corpus credibly
clean). A **decoy companion** (`...-decoys` config) shipping flagged decoys
+ scores is a v0.2 add-on for FDR recomputation / rescorer training (§10).

### 3.3 Fragment / ion-level keep (Tier 3) — one canonical row per fragment

- **Keep every matched b/y fragment either engine reports.** No intensity
  threshold at build (intensity is the label; a cutoff biases targets).
- **One canonical row per `(raw_file, precursor_id, fragment_type, ordinal,
  fragment_charge)`** — NOT three rows (sage / fragpipe / both). Naive
  `sage`+`fragpipe`+`both` rows triple-count agreed fragments and inflate
  intensity sums. Each canonical row carries:
  `sage_matched`, `fragpipe_matched` (bool), `sage_mz_exp`,
  `fragpipe_mz_exp`, `sage_intensity`, `fragpipe_intensity`,
  `fragment_mz_calculated`, `agreement_ppm` (|Δ| between the two when both),
  `match_method`.
- **Matcher symmetry (RESOLVED 2026-06-10 → option (a)).** The cross-engine
  agreement label is built from **imspy re-matching BOTH engines' peptide
  assignments under identical settings** — so `sage_matched`/`fragpipe_matched`
  + `agreement_ppm` measure true **peptide-assignment agreement**, not the
  rematcher. Requires running `extract_fragment_peaks.py --peptide-source
  sage` **and** `--peptide-source fragpipe` across the cohort. Sage's
  **native** annotations (`matched_fragments.sage.parquet`) are kept as a
  **separate provenance** pair of columns (`sage_native_matched`,
  `sage_native_intensity`), NOT folded into the agreement label — so a user
  can compare imspy-vs-Sage-native matching if they want, but the headline
  agreement is matcher-consistent.
- **Extractor must be reworked first** (existing outputs unusable, §1):
  `extract_fragment_peaks.py` currently filters `n_engines≥2 &
  is_high_quality` (L753–754), falls back to `consensus_peptide`
  (L288–313 — can falsely report an engine match), and `_find_peak` matches
  each theoretical ion independently (one peak can satisfy several ions —
  NOT one-to-one); unmatched ions are `mz=0` not null. Rerun **both** passes
  **without** the quality gate and **without** the consensus fallback.
- **Frozen one-to-one matching:** within a precursor, form all ion↔peak
  candidates inside the ppm tolerance, sort by
  `(abs_ppm, desc_peak_intensity, ion_type, ordinal, charge, peak_index)`,
  and **greedily consume each ion and each peak exactly once**. Mono masses,
  proton convention, documented NL/isotope handling. Unmatched → null (not 0).
- **Canonical fragment key requires same peptidoform:** merge a Sage row and
  a FragPipe row under one canonical fragment key **only when their
  normalized peptidoform AND precursor charge are identical** — `b3` of
  peptide A is not `b3` of peptide B, so for `peptidoform_conflict` rows
  emit **separate assignment rows** (include assignment identity in the key).
  Else `agreement_ppm` is chemically meaningless.
- **Frozen `agreement_ppm`** = `1e6 · |sage_mz_exp − fragpipe_mz_exp| /
  fragment_mz_calculated`, **null unless both engines matched the same
  theoretical ion** of the same peptidoform.
- **b/y only for v1.** No NL / immonium / internal ions — the imspy
  extension work showed they carry ~0 information beyond b/y on tryptic data
  (reverse-decoy null) and would balloon Tier 3. Future, conditional on a
  PTM-rich retest (`[[imspy-fragment-ion-extension]]`).

### 3.4 Engine agreement — a defined label, not a gate

Null on a per-engine field currently conflates six cases (engine not run /
artifact missing / no candidate / not rank-1 / failed q / different
peptidoform). Disambiguate with **per-engine status columns**:

```
sage_searched, sage_candidate_present, sage_rank, sage_passes_floor
fragpipe_searched, fragpipe_candidate_present, fragpipe_rank, fragpipe_passes_floor
```

**Grain-coherent definition (corrected v5).** A Tier-1 row is ONE accepted
precursor identification, so it carries ONE canonical peptidoform — it cannot
hold two engines' conflicting assignments as separate accepted PSMs, and
`n_engines` is a scalar (it can't "count them separately"). Rules:
- Pick the **canonical assignment** for the row (Sage-preferred-else-FragPipe,
  §4.0). `charge` and the canonical sequence come from the **same** engine —
  never mixed across engines.
- `n_engines` = number of engines whose passing assignment has the **same
  normalized peptidoform + charge** as the canonical one (1 or 2).
- Ship `sage_assignment_passes_floor` / `fragpipe_assignment_passes_floor`
  (bool) and `peptidoform_conflict` (true when both engines pass the floor but
  assign different peptidoforms) — the disagreement is preserved as a flag,
  not by mixing columns.
- We **do not drop** single-engine PSMs (HLA / PTM are often single-engine);
  users wanting agreement filter `n_engines == 2`.

### 3.5 The documented "strict" preset

One named, reproducible high-confidence subset, one boolean away:

```
strict := (n_engines == 2)                       # same peptidoform+charge, both pass floor
        & (sage_qvalue <= 0.01) & (fragpipe_qvalue <= 0.01)
        & rt_width_reliable & im_width_reliable   # §3.6
```

Materialized as a `strict` column + documented in the card. (This is the
v2_strict training subset, expressed without deleting anything. Note it
does NOT inherit a nominal FDR — §3.2.)

### 3.6 Width-label reliability (from the shape investigation)

The peak-shape investigation found **fit R² alone is insufficient** — σ
recovery degrades fast below SNR ~10 even when R² looks fine. So ship,
**separately for each axis**:
- `ms1_rt_r2`, `ms1_im_r2` (goodness of fit),
- `ms1_rt_snr`, `ms1_im_snr` (apex-over-baseline-noise),
- `ms1_rt_fit_status`, `ms1_im_fit_status` (enum: `ok` / `failed` /
  `not_extracted` — so null distinguishes *missing extraction* from
  *failed fit*),
- `rt_width_reliable`, `im_width_reliable` (per-axis bools), and the
  convenience `width_reliable = rt_width_reliable & im_width_reliable`.

`ms1_*_snr` is computed in the Tier-1 blob pass (§4.1) from the MS1 RT/IM
trace. **FROZEN estimator `trace_snr_v1`** (version it in the manifest):
1. reject non-finite coords/intensities; clamp negative intensities to 0;
2. require ≥7 trace points and ≥3 points on each side of the fitted apex;
3. peak-exclusion interval = apex ± `2.5·σ`; **reject (null) truncated traces**
   where that interval reaches either trace boundary;
4. baseline samples = points **outside** the exclusion interval;
5. `baseline = median(baseline_samples)`;
6. `noise_sigma = 1.4826 · median(|samples − baseline|)`;
7. `snr = max(0, apex_intensity − baseline) / noise_sigma`;
8. if <6 baseline points, `noise_sigma ≤ 0`, fit failed, or truncated →
   SNR **null** + a specific `*_fit_status`, **never infinity**;
9. **no smoothing** before baseline/noise estimation.
**Two estimators (validated 2026-06-10 on PXD019086, n=3000):** the
baseline-region `trace_snr_v1` above works for **IM mobilograms** (50–200 pts;
2321/3000 ok, median SNR 42) but is **undefined for timsTOF RT XICs** (7–13
pts — the peak fills the trace, so apex±2.5σ hits a boundary: only 5/3000 ok).
→ RT uses **`trace_snr_v1_rt`**: require ≥7 points + a clear interior apex;
noise = `1.4826·MAD` of the **lowest ⌊n/3⌋ intensities** (baseline floor), with
a successive-difference fallback when that floor is flat; null on edge-apex
(1883/3000 ok, median 12.8). Manifest records `snr_version_rt` /
`snr_version_im` separately. Implemented in `scripts/build_hf_tier1.py`.

⚠️ The `SNR ≥ 20 → ≤6% σ error` band stays **provisional** until revalidated
beyond the PXD019086-frac01 probe (the band feeds `*_width_reliable`: at this
threshold ~16% of RT and ~54% of IM widths are flagged reliable). Ship
`ms1_*_snr` as a number and `*_width_reliable` as a documented-provisional
flag; state provenance + instrument/intensity range in the card.

**Units:** RT in seconds, IM in 1/K0 (state both in the card). Do **not**
impute `gradient_length` — keep it null when unknown; an imputed value goes
in a separate `gradient_length_imputed` column with its source, never
silently into the calibration field (§4).

## 4. Schema

Three-tier design; **first release ships Tier 1 + Tier 3** (Tier 2 deferred).

### 4.0 Grain & source schema — VERIFIED on MOGON (2026-06-10)

Inspected 5 datasets (`/lustre/.../inspect_grain.py`, srun ki-smallcpu):

- **Grain CONFIRMED: per-precursor, keyed on `(raw_file, precursor_id)`.**
  `precursor_index.parquet` and `raw_features.parquet` are each 1-row-per
  `(raw_file, precursor_id)` and align **exactly row-for-row** (identical
  num_rows: 214 824 / 1 388 933 / 2 387 890 / 494 391). Tier 1 = a clean
  **inner join on `(raw_file, precursor_id)`** — no PSM-collapse logic
  needed. So `psm_id`/`n_observations`/`frame_id` from the v3/v4 draft are
  dropped; the stable PSM handle is **`sage_psm_id`** (FragPipe exposes none
  at this grain). Acquisition coords (frame/scan) live only in the blob
  `index.parquet` (Tier 2), not here.
- ⚠️ **ANOMALY — PXD062839**: `precursor_index` has 5 186 278 rows but only
  2 861 875 unique `(raw_file, precursor_id)` (duplicate keys) **and** its
  row count disagrees with `raw_features` (7 971 094). A per-group merge
  artifact (cf. `[[per-group-merge-bug]]`). → the build needs a **1:1
  alignment gate** (§8) that quarantines/flags any dataset whose two tables
  don't align uniquely; never silently inner-join a duplicated key.
- **Schema is dataset-variable.** FragPipe-only datasets (e.g. PXD012981,
  compression-type-1, excluded anyway) **lack all `sage_*` columns** (19
  cols vs 32). The build must **union-schema + null-fill**, not assume a
  fixed column set.
- **Real column names (vs the draft's guesses):**
  - Confidence IS present per engine: `sage_qvalue`, `sage_pep`,
    `sage_hyperscore`, `sage_psm_id`, **plus** `sage_peptide_qvalue`,
    `sage_protein_qvalue`, and `sage_cosine` (a spectral-cosine quality
    signal — ship it); `fragpipe_qvalue/pep/probability/hyperscore`.
  - **No unified `sequence`/`modified_sequence`** — peptidoform is
    per-engine (`sage_peptide`/`sage_modified`, `fragpipe_peptide`/
    `fragpipe_modified`) + `sequence_normalized`. Define a canonical
    peptidoform (prefer Sage when present, else FragPipe) AND keep both.
  - Width labels present: `ms1_{rt,im}_{apex,fwhm,sigma,skew,r2}` (the
    Gaussian-LSQ σ — document it over-widens for tailing RT peaks, §3.6).
  - **NO `ms1_*_snr` and NO per-event `collision_energies` list.**
    raw_features has only scalar `collision_energy` (per-precursor mean).
    Both headline-novel columns require decoding **`blobs.bin`** (pointers
    `blob_offset`/`blob_size` are in raw_features) — i.e. a Tier-2-class
    pass. **Decision pending (§10):** compute them in the Tier-1 build via a
    blob pass, or ship scalar CE + a cheap intensity proxy for v0.1 and
    defer true per-event CE + trace-SNR.
  - Bonus columns to ship: `sage_cosine`, `fragment_im_apex/fwhm`,
    `fragment_{mz,im}_{mean,var}`, `mono_mz`, `isolation_mz/width`.

### 4.1 Tier-1 build is a full blob pass (decision (a), 2026-06-10)

Because `ms1_*_snr` and per-event `collision_energies_v` are computed from
`blobs.bin` (not the parquets), **the Tier-1 build is a per-dataset Slurm
batch job that reads every precursor's blob**, not a cheap parquet join.
Consequences:
- **Scale:** **full 6.4 TB compressed I/O** — each precursor blob is
  separately outer-compressed, so any access decompresses the whole blob.
  This is NOT "a fraction"; the saving is only that we don't *materialize*
  the raw 4D cloud, not that we skip decompressing it.
- **Projection reader — do NOT use `BlobReader.read_batch()`** (it
  materializes the full blob, eagerly loads fragment + MS1 + raw-4D arrays,
  retains every `RawSignal` in a list, and silently nulls decode errors →
  memory blowup). Write a projection reader: process **one blob at a time**;
  from the lazy NPZ load access **only** `ms1_rt_coords/intensities` +
  `ms1_im_coords/intensities` + the JSON metadata (never touch `raw_*`/
  `frag_*`); compute SNR + CE immediately; release buffers. Sort rows by
  `(raw_file, blob_offset)` for sequential reads; open each raw file once.
- **Robustness:** validate `offset≥0, size>0, offset+size≤file_size`; assert
  blob-metadata `precursor_id` == the parquet row; **count decode failures
  by reason and quarantine the dataset above a frozen threshold — never
  silent-null**; write **incremental Parquet row groups** (not an
  accession-wide pandas frame); checkpoint per raw file with atomic rename +
  input checksums; size Slurm **concurrency by FS throughput** and **memory
  by the max uncompressed blob**, not averages.
- **SNR:** `trace_snr_v1` (§3.6), computed separately for RT and IM from the
  same traces the FWHM labels came from.
- **Per-event CE:** decode the per-PASEF-event CE list from blob metadata →
  `collision_energies_v` + `collision_energy_mean_v`, in **raw volts** (the
  existing extraction logs mislabel CE as "eV" — it is volts). (Probe the
  metadata key on a sample blob before wiring — §10.)
- The projection decode is shared with the deferred Tier-2 build (not
  throwaway).

are computed in the blob pass (§4.1).

## 6. Splits (group-aware, leakage-safe — corrected algorithm)

A naive "hash by `sequence_normalized`" **and** "force whole `group_id`s
into one split" are **mutually impossible** (a sequence appears in many
groups; a group holds many sequences). Correct construction:

1. Build the **bipartite graph** linking `sequence_normalized` ↔ `group_id`.
2. Take its **connected components** — each component is an indivisible
   leakage unit (shares either peptides or experimental origin).
3. Assign whole components to train/val/test (target 80/10/10 by PSM count,
   workflow-stratified where feasible).
4. **Apply canonical holdouts first** (PXD019086 (Meier 2021), + consider
   1 HLA + 1 phospho), then fill the rest.
5. Ship a `split` column **and publish the component-assignment algorithm +
   seed** in the card (a seed alone doesn't define the partition).

**Leakage caveats to document:** split on `sequence_normalized` (unmod) so
modified variants of one backbone don't straddle splits; exact raw-file
fingerprinting will **not** catch format-converted copies, renamed
resubmissions, or repeated biological acquisitions across PXDs — the
`group_id` graph (DOI / lab+submitter+year / sample) is the real guard, the
fingerprint only catches byte-duplicates.

## 7. Versioning & reproducibility (semantic + immutable manifest)

**Scheme:** one **unversioned** HF repo per artifact, versions as **immutable
git revision tags** (HF datasets are git repos) — NOT the version baked into
the repo name (that complicates semantic evolution and cross-version refs):
- repos: `claudius-proteomics/timstof-psms-cc0`,
  `claudius-proteomics/timstof-fragments-cc0`
  (`...-traces-cc0` later for Tier 2).
- tags: `v0.1` (Tier 1+3, private first), `v0.2` (+cohort / decoys / fixes),
  `v1.0` (public, + Tier 2 in traces repo).
- **First *public* release** = whenever we flip the repo public (likely at
  `v1.0`), independent of the first *tag*. State this explicitly so "first
  release" (v0.1, private) and "first public" (v1.0) don't read as
  contradictory.
- bump rules: patch = card/metadata only; minor = cohort/schema additions;
  major = tier addition or breaking schema change. Tags are never moved.

**Manifest** (`manifest.json`, in every revision + summarized in the card) —
must be sufficient to **reproduce the release**, so beyond `corpus_version`
+ `build_pipeline_git_sha` + `schema_version` + `license`, record:
- **Checksums:** SHA-256 + size for every **input artifact** and every
  **output shard**; per-shard row counts.
- **Source provenance:** raw-file fingerprints; PRIDE source URLs +
  retrieval dates; a **PRIDE metadata snapshot** (not a live-API dependency).
- **Tool versions:** Sage, FragPipe, MSBooster/PeptideProphet, timsrust,
  imspy, sagepy_rescore — pinned versions + the container/env digest.
- **Search config:** full Sage/FragPipe configs, FASTA checksum, contaminant
  DB, decoy-generation method.
- **Extraction + filter params:** extraction settings; `q_max=0.01`,
  `rank==1`, target-only; fragment ppm tolerance + match spec (§3.3); SNR
  definition version (§3.6); split algorithm + seed (§6).
- **Schema doc:** every column with **units + null semantics**.

Per-dataset manifest rows: accession, workflow, engines available,
`gradient_length` + source (or null), PSM counts (pre-floor / post-floor /
n_engines==2), CE tier, group_id + connected-component id, inclusion
outcome + reason.

## 8. Build pipeline

Scripts under `scripts/`, MOGON-side (`build_hf_tier*.py` **not yet written**
— first implementation step after this plan lands).

1. **Refresh inventory** (§9, one-shot SSH, *no polling*): re-tally
   dual-engine + raw_features + fragment-peaks; regenerate manifest rows.
2. ✅ **Row grain confirmed** (§4.0): per-precursor, `(raw_file,
   precursor_id)`, 1:1 with raw_features. Tier 1 = inner join on that key.
3. **`scripts/build_hf_tier1.py`** — **Slurm array, one job per accession,
   full blob pass (§4.1)**: inner-join `precursor_index` + `raw_features` on
   `(raw_file, precursor_id)` (after the 1:1 gate); union-schema + null-fill
   so FP-only datasets don't break; pick the canonical peptidoform
   (Sage-else-FragPipe, keep per-engine); read each precursor's blob
   (`blob_offset/size` → `BlobReader`) to compute `ms1_*_snr` and per-event
   `collision_energies_v`/`collision_energy_mean_v`; apply the §3.2 floor;
   compute per-engine status, `n_engines`/`peptidoform_conflict`,
   `*_fit_status`, `*_width_reliable`, `strict`; write `hf_tier1/<acc>.parquet`.
4. **Fragment prerequisite (matcher-symmetric, §3.3)** — run BOTH
   `extract_fragment_peaks.py --peptide-source sage` and `--peptide-source
   fragpipe` across the cohort (Slurm array) so imspy matches each engine's
   peptide under identical settings. Sage-native
   `matched_fragments.sage.parquet` already exists → used as the separate
   provenance source.
5. **`scripts/build_hf_tier3.py`** — per accession: union the two imspy
   rematch outputs (sage-peptide, fragpipe-peptide) into the **canonical
   one-row-per-fragment** table (§3.3 schema) with deterministic one-to-one
   matching + `agreement_ppm`; left-join Sage-native annotations as the
   `sage_native_*` provenance columns; write `hf_tier3/<acc>.parquet`.
6. **Aggregate + split** — concat per-acc → shard ~1 GB; assign `split` via
   the §6 connected-component algorithm; write `manifest.json` with all §7
   provenance.
7. **Validation gates** (pre-upload): **1:1 alignment gate** (precursor_index
   ↔ raw_features unique-key match per dataset — quarantine PXD062839-class
   anomalies, §4.0); schema/dtype + units; floor assertion
   (no sub-floor survivors); q-level assertion (PSM-level, §3.2);
   referential integrity (every Tier-3 precursor in Tier 1); fragment
   one-to-one (no many-to-many); width-reliability distribution sanity;
   manifest checksum completeness. (No CE `[0,1]` assertion — we ship raw
   volts; range-sanity is "plausible Bruker V," not a unit interval.)
8. **Dataset card** (`README.md`): cohort table; schema with units/null
   semantics; **filtering policy (§3) stated plainly incl. the FDR-union
   caveat**; `strict` recipe; split algorithm; per-PXD DOI citations;
   license = CC0 with per-dataset evidence.
9. **Upload** — `push_to_hub(..., private=True)`; tag the revision; flip
   public after card review.
